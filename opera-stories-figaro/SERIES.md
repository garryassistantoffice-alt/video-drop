# Le nozze di Figaro as a series: one plot twist per episode, every episode ends on a cliffhanger

Channel: **Opera Stories** (Alex's decision, 2026-10-15).

Alex, 2026-10-15: "promote it as a fast way to understand what the opera is about"; the trailer-like Short was too
vague. So: 12 episodes of 70-80 seconds (Alex: up to 1:20), each explaining one turn of the plot clearly (who
wants what, and why), naming the one famous number in it (Italian title + what it says), and ending on a
cliffhanger with "Next episode: ...". Facts checked against the Italian libretto (it.wikisource.org, all four acts re-read
on 2026-10-15). Replaces the 5-Short split of PLAN.md.

Pictures: numbers 1-9 are the texts in short1-pictures.md (Alex has 1-8); letters A-H are new texts in
pictures-act1.md (episodes 1-4) and pictures-act2.md (episodes 5-7, I-S); T-JJ still to be written. About 4-5
pictures per episode, several reused with other crops.

## Scripts for episodes 2-4 (draft, Act I re-read in the libretto)
Each about 185-195 words, about 75 s with the Kokoro voice at speed 1.12.

### 2. The contract
- (5) The Marriage of Figaro, episode two: the contract.
- (5) Marcellina, the palace housekeeper, once lent Figaro money. His signed promise: pay it back, or marry her. And today he marries Susanna.
- (5) Her plan is clever: frighten Susanna into refusing the Count. The Count, offended, will take Marcellina's side, and Figaro will be hers.
- (B) Her partner is old Doctor Bartolo. Why does he hate Figaro? In The Barber of Seville, Figaro helped the Count steal Bartolo's young ward, Rosina, who is now the Countess.
- (B) So Bartolo sings La vendetta: revenge is a pleasure for the wise. He will search every law book for a trick, because all of Seville knows Bartolo.
- (C) Then Marcellina meets Susanna in the doorway. A duel of curtsies: after you, madam bride! No, after you, madam lady! Your position, says one. Your age, says the other. Marcellina storms off.
- (6) Susanna is still laughing when someone rushes in: Cherubino, the Count's teenage page, in love with every woman in the palace. And he is in trouble.
- (6) Next episode: the boy behind the chair.

### 3. The boy behind the chair
- (6) The Marriage of Figaro, episode three: the boy behind the chair.
- (6) Yesterday the Count found Cherubino alone with Barbarina, the gardener's daughter, and sacked him. Now he begs Susanna: ask the Countess to save me.
- (E) He adores the Countess. He snatches her ribbon from Susanna's hand and sings Non so più: I don't know what I am any more; every woman makes my heart race.
- (F) Footsteps: the Count! Cherubino hides behind the big armchair. The Count sits, takes Susanna's hand and asks her to meet him in the garden at dusk.
- (A) Then Basilio's voice outside. Now the Count hides behind the chair; the boy slips round onto the seat, and Susanna throws a dress over him.
- (A) Basilio gossips: everyone knows the page is in love with the Countess. The Count jumps out. What does everyone say?
- (7) Yesterday at Barbarina's, he says, I lifted the tablecloth, like this, and found the page! He lifts the dress. There is the page, again.
- (7) Worse: the boy was there the whole time, and heard everything the Count said to Susanna. Next episode: off to the army.

### 4. Off to the army
- (G) The Marriage of Figaro, episode four: off to the army.
- (G) Figaro arrives with villagers dressed in white, throwing flowers: thank you, noble Count, for abolishing the old right!
- (H) It's a trap. Figaro holds out a white veil: please, sir, put it on my bride yourself, here, in front of everyone.
- (H) The Count can't refuse in public. He smiles, praises them, and asks for a little delay, for a grander ceremony. Quietly he adds: find Marcellina.
- (8) And the page who heard too much? The Count forgives him, and makes him an officer in his regiment. He must leave at once.
- (8) Figaro whispers to the boy: I must talk to you before you go. Then he teases him with Non più andrai: no more fluttering about, little butterfly. Now it's moustaches, muskets, and marching through the mud.
- (9) But Cherubino is not leaving the palace. Upstairs, the Countess is alone and heartbroken. Next episode: the Countess's plan.

## Scripts for episodes 5-12 (draft, Acts II-IV re-read in the libretto on 2026-10-15)

### 5. The Countess's plan
- (9) The Marriage of Figaro, episode five: the Countess's plan.
- (9) Upstairs, the Countess is alone. Years ago the Count climbed balconies for her. Now he chases her maid. She sings Porgi, amor: love, give me back my husband, or let me die.
- (I) Figaro has a plan. He has sent the Count an anonymous letter: tonight, during the dance, the Countess meets a lover. Let the jealous husband worry about his own wife for once.
- (I) And Susanna will promise to meet the Count in the garden at dusk. But it won't be Susanna who goes. It will be Cherubino, in a dress, and the Countess will catch her husband in the act.
- (J) The Count is out hunting, so there is time. Cherubino arrives and sings his song for the Countess, Voi che sapete: you ladies who know what love is, tell me, is this love?
- (K) Susanna dresses him up: a lady's cap, eyes down, hands just so. The Countess notices that his officer's commission has no seal yet.
- (L) Then, a knock. The Count is back early, with the letter. And in his wife's locked room stands a boy with half his clothes off.
- (L) Next episode: the locked closet.

### 6. The locked closet
- (M) The Marriage of Figaro, episode six: the locked closet.
- (M) Cherubino runs into the Countess's dressing closet. Pale, she lets her husband in. Why was the door locked? Then a crash from the closet: the boy has knocked over a chair.
- (M) Who is in there? Only Susanna, says the Countess, trying on her wedding dress. Then let her speak! The Countess forbids it.
- (M) The Count has had enough. He will break the door down. He locks every door, and takes his wife with him to fetch the tools, so nobody can slip away.
- (N) But Susanna saw it all from the alcove. The moment they leave, she lets the boy out. The only way out is the window, above the gardener's flowerbeds. Cherubino jumps.
- (N) Susanna locks herself in the closet in his place.
- (O) The Count returns with a hammer and pliers. Now the Countess confesses: it's Cherubino, half undressed, but innocent, just a harmless joke.
- (O) The Count snatches the key. Let him die! He turns to the door. Next episode: the door opens.

### 7. The door opens
- (P) The Marriage of Figaro, episode seven: the door opens.
- (P) Out steps Susanna. Calm, smiling: my lord, why so surprised? The Count is lost. The Countess is just as stunned, but quickly plays along.
- (P) Now the Count must beg forgiveness. Rosina, he says, her name from the old days. And the letter about a lover? Figaro wrote it, the women admit. Only a joke.
- (Q) Figaro bursts in: the music is ready, let's have the wedding! Then Antonio the gardener, Susanna's uncle, half drunk, with a smashed pot of carnations: a man jumped from this balcony onto my flowers!
- (Q) It was me, says Figaro, rubbing his ankle. Then you dropped this paper, says Antonio. The Count grabs it. What is it?
- (R) The women whisper the answer: Cherubino's officer's commission. And why did you have it? Because, says Figaro, it needed the seal. Saved again.
- (S) But then Marcellina, Bartolo and Basilio march in with the contract, and the Count smiles. He will judge the case himself.
- (S) Next episode: the trap in the garden.

### 8. The trap in the garden
- (T) The Marriage of Figaro, episode eight: the trap in the garden.
- (T) The Count paces. A locked closet, a letter, a jump from a window. Is he being played?
- (U) The Countess has a new plan, and this one she keeps even from Figaro. Susanna will promise the Count a meeting in the garden tonight, but the Countess herself will go, in Susanna's clothes.
- (V) Susanna finds the Count. Will she come? Yes, she says, if he gives her the dowry he promised, so she can pay off Marcellina. Their duet is Crudel, perché finora: cruel girl, why make me wait so long?
- (V) He is delighted. But leaving, Susanna whispers to Figaro: you've already won your case.
- (T) The Count hears it. A trap! In Vedrò mentr'io sospiro he rages: shall I sigh while my servant is happy? Never. Figaro will marry Marcellina.
- (W) And the judge, Don Curzio, is ready with the verdict.
- (W) Next episode: Mother!

### 9. Mother!
- (W) The Marriage of Figaro, episode nine: Mother!
- (W) The verdict: pay her or marry her. Two thousand silver pieces, and Figaro doesn't have them.
- (X) One last trick: I'm a gentleman, he says. I can't marry without my noble parents' consent. Robbers stole me as a baby, dressed in gold, jewels and embroidery. And I have a mark on my arm.
- (X) A little spatula, on the right arm? asks Marcellina. How do you know? Because you are my son, Raffaello. And your father is standing right there: Doctor Bartolo.
- (Y) Just then Susanna runs in with a purse: a thousand gold coins to set Figaro free. And she sees him hugging Marcellina. Slap!
- (Y) Your mother? His mother! In the famous sextet everyone explains, Susanna laughs, and Marcellina and Bartolo decide to marry as well. Two weddings today.
- (U) The Count leaves, beaten. But tonight his wife will wait for him in the garden in Susanna's dress, and Figaro knows nothing about it.
- (U) Next episode: the letter with the pin.

### 10. The letter with the pin
- (Z) The Marriage of Figaro, episode ten: the letter with the pin.
- (Z) The Countess, alone, remembers: Dove sono, where are the beautiful moments of sweetness and love? She will win her husband back.
- (AA) She dictates, Susanna writes, Sull'aria: what a gentle breeze tonight, under the pines. The letter is sealed with a pin; the Count must send the pin back as his answer.
- (BB) Village girls bring flowers to the Countess. Among them a shy one: Cherubino in a dress. Antonio pulls off the bonnet. Caught again!
- (BB) The Count wants to punish him, but Barbarina speaks up: you kissed me, sir, and promised me whatever I want. I want Cherubino as my husband. The Count is trapped by his own promises.
- (CC) The double wedding begins with a fandango. Kneeling for her bridal wreath, Susanna slips the Count the letter. He pricks his finger on the pin, drops it, then picks it up and pins it to his sleeve.
- (CC) Figaro sees and laughs: some girl has sent the Count a love note. He has no idea it was his bride.
- (CC) Next episode: Figaro's jealousy.

### 11. Figaro's jealousy
- (DD) The Marriage of Figaro, episode eleven: Figaro's jealousy.
- (DD) Night. Barbarina searches the garden with a paper lantern. She has lost the pin the Count gave her for Susanna, with a message: the seal of the pines.
- (EE) Figaro hears it, and his world falls apart. His bride, on their wedding day, has a meeting with the Count.
- (EE) Marcellina doubts it, but Figaro won't listen. He hides among the pines to catch them, and sings Aprite un po' quegli occhi: men, open your eyes and see what women really are.
- (FF) Susanna and the Countess arrive, already in each other's clothes. Marcellina has warned Susanna that Figaro is hiding nearby.
- (FF) So Susanna teases him. In Deh vieni, non tardar, one of the loveliest arias Mozart wrote, she sings: come, my love, don't be late. Figaro thinks it is for the Count. It is for him.
- (FF) Next episode, the last one: Contessa, perdono.

### 12. Contessa, perdono
- (GG) The Marriage of Figaro, the last episode: Contessa, perdono.
- (GG) In the dark the Countess waits in Susanna's dress. Cherubino finds her, takes her for Susanna, and tries to steal a kiss.
- (GG) The Count steps between them and gets the kiss himself. He swings at the boy; the slap lands on Figaro, creeping closer.
- (HH) Then the Count woos "Susanna", really his own wife, and gives her a ring.
- (HH) Figaro meets "the Countess", but he knows that voice: it's Susanna. He plays along and declares his love to "my lady". She slaps him, then they laugh and make up.
- (II) The Count sees Figaro at the feet of "his wife" and explodes. He calls everyone out. Everyone begs: forgive! No, no, no, he shouts.
- (JJ) Then the real Countess steps forward. The Count understands, and kneels: Contessa, perdono, Countess, forgive me. She does: I am kinder than you, and I say yes.
- (JJ) The crazy day ends in a party. That was The Marriage of Figaro.

## Useful for people and for AI (Alex, 2026-10-25)
Every episode gets a companion pack, made by `episodes/pack.py` from the same script (run in `episodes/epNN` with
`info.json` and the build's `timings.json`):
- `pack/captions.srt`: the exact narration with times, uploaded as YouTube subtitles (better than automatic ones;
  search and AI tools read captions).
- `pack/youtube.txt`: title, description with chapters (each at least 10 s), the famous number with its meaning,
  characters with voice types, the source, the full narration, and a plain note that voice and paintings are
  AI-made (YouTube asks for that label on realistic AI content).
- `pack/page.html`: an episode page for Alex's website (Perry): the text, questions and answers, and schema.org
  VideoObject + FAQPage data, which search engines and AI assistants read to cite a page.
- `pack/facts.json`: the same facts as plain data, for an "Opera Stories" index later.
