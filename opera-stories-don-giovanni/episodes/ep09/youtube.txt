Full narration
Don Giovanni, episode nine: the statue. A walled burial ground, a night of moonlight brighter than the day. Don Giovanni has jumped over the wall to escape. Leporello finds him there, still in each other's clothes. His master is laughing: tonight, in the street, a girl took him for Leporello and called him darling. What if it was my wife? cries Leporello. Better still, laughs Don Giovanni, louder. Ah ah ah, this is a good one. A voice from the darkness: Di rider finirai pria dell'aurora. You will stop laughing before the dawn. Insolent wretch, the voice goes on, leave the dead in peace. Who spoke? Among the tombs stands a white marble statue, armoured, a stone sword in its hand: the Commendatore. The lantern shakes in Leporello's hand. Trembling, he holds it up and reads the words carved on its base: here I await revenge on the wicked man who brought me to my death. Leporello wants only to run. Don Giovanni laughs at the old fool. Tell him, he orders Leporello, that I invite him to supper tonight. Leporello refuses, until his master's sword is at his back. Shaking, he bows to the stone. O statua gentilissima: oh most noble statue. He cannot finish the sentence. The statue nods its head. Did you see? Don Giovanni does not believe it. He speaks to it himself. Will you come to supper? And the stone answers: Sì. Even Don Giovanni stops for a moment. Strange indeed, he says. Then he shrugs. Let us go home and prepare the supper.
